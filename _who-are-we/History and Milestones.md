<p>Since its establishment, the Lee Kuan Yew Fund for Bilingualism has worked
to strengthen Singapore’s bilingual foundation by supporting high-quality,
locally relevant resources and programmes in English and the Mother Tongue
Languages.</p>
<p>Early childhood remains central to the Fund’s mission. The formative years
are an important period for language acquisition, and children are more
likely to develop a lasting interest in languages when they encounter them
through familiar stories, songs, games and everyday experiences.</p>
<p>Over time, the Fund’s work has expanded beyond the preschool setting.
We support parents and caregivers in creating language-rich homes, bring
bilingualism into community and cultural spaces, and engage youths and
young adults through media, technology and popular culture. This reflects
our belief that bilingualism is not simply a school subject, but a lifelong
relationship with language, culture and identity.</p>
<p>We work with educators, researchers, artists, publishers, media organisations
and community partners to catalyse initiatives with clear and substantive
bilingual outcomes. Some examples include:</p>
<h3><strong>Building Strong Early Foundations</strong></h3>
<p>The Fund has supported the development of locally contextualised books,
songs, games and learning materials that allow young children to encounter
English and their Mother Tongue Languages in engaging and meaningful ways.</p>
<p>Among the Fund’s early supported resources were <em>Thumbs Up Little Junior</em>,
introduced in 2013 as the first Chinese-language print publication dedicated
to preschool children; <em>Cilik Cerdik</em> in Malay, introduced in 2014;
and <em>Balar Murasu</em>in Tamil, introduced in 2017. Together, these publications
widened access to age-appropriate Mother Tongue Language content across
the three language communities.</p>
<p>Another notable initiative was <em>Junction Tree</em>, Singapore’s first
locally produced bilingual preschool television programme. Produced in
English-Chinese, English-Malay and English-Tamil versions, the programme
received the Best Preschool Programme award at the 2018 Asian Academy Creative
Awards.</p>
<h3>Supporting Families and Educators</h3>
<p>Recognising that children’s earliest experiences with language begin at
home, the Fund launched the <em>Love Our Languages!</em> Community Series
in 2014. Through practical workshops and activities, the series equips
parents and caregivers with strategies to incorporate Mother Tongue Languages
into everyday conversations, storytelling and play. It has since developed
into one of the Fund’s longest-running community initiatives.</p>
<p>The Fund also participates in national platforms such as the Mother Tongue
Languages Symposium and Early Childhood Celebrations and Conferences. These
engagements bring together families, educators, researchers and community
partners to exchange ideas, discover useful resources and explore effective
approaches to bilingual learning.</p>
<h3><strong>Enabling Ground-up Ideas</strong></h3>
<p>Through its Call for Proposals, the Fund provides opportunities for educators,
artists, publishers, researchers and content creators to contribute to
Singapore’s bilingual landscape.</p>
<p>What began largely with preschool teaching and learning resources has
grown to include books, digital tools, performances and community programmes.
During the COVID-19 pandemic, the <em>#IAmBilingualSG</em> Special Call supported
the development of digital resources that enabled families to continue
engaging with languages at home.</p>
<h3><strong>Connecting Language, Culture and Identity</strong></h3>
<p>Language carries more than meaning. It also carries memories, values,
traditions and a sense of belonging. The Fund therefore supports initiatives
that allow bilingualism to be experienced through heritage, performance,
storytelling and the arts.</p>
<p>These include <em>New World. New Life</em>, a bilingual and multisensory
exhibition presented by the Singapore Federation of Chinese Clan Associations
to commemorate the Singapore Bicentennial. Collaborations with cultural
and community organisations have similarly brought language into spaces
where culture is lived, shared and remembered.</p>
<p>By situating language within familiar food, neighbourhoods, celebrations,
stories and cultural practices, these initiatives help Singaporeans see
their Mother Tongue Languages not merely as subjects to be learnt, but
as an integral part of who they are.</p>
<h3><strong>Engaging Youths and Young Adults</strong></h3>
<p>As Singapore’s linguistic landscape changes, the Fund has adopted new
formats to make bilingualism relevant to younger audiences. Partnerships
with tertiary institutions, media organisations and content creators have
introduced bilingualism through short-form videos, social media, reality
programmes and live experiences.</p>
<p>A collaboration with Temasek Polytechnic produced ten short videos presenting
bilingualism as a dynamic part of young people’s lives. More recent productions
include <em>The Finish Line</em>, a six-episode reality series in which
participants applied their bilingual abilities to real-world challenges,
and <em>Qrious Culture</em>, which explored identity and personal growth
through video podcasts and a live showcase.</p>
<p>These initiatives invite young people to become active participants in
shaping how bilingualism is understood and expressed.</p>
<h3><strong>Connecting Research with Practice</strong></h3>
<p>The Fund’s work is also informed by research and professional exchange.
In 2013, it supported four major conferences, including the 9th International
Symposium on Bilingualism and the inaugural East Asia Summit Conference
on Bilingualism.</p>
<p>The Fund’s Research Advisory Panel, established in its present form in
2019, continues to provide expert guidance on relevant research and projects.
This helps ensure that supported initiatives remain evidence-based, responsive
and grounded in Singapore’s linguistic context.</p>
<p>Guided by the vision of <strong>“Bilingualism as Our Identity, Our Lives, Our Advantage”</strong>,
the Fund will continue to inspire a love for languages, catalyse meaningful
partnerships and celebrate the individuals and communities that keep bilingualism
alive across generations.</p>
